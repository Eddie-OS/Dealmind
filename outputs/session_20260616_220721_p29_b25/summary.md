### 有效策略
- 第5轮 [Live stress test demonstration]：Directly addressed the customer's technical concerns with tangible proof, leveraging the in-store environment to build trust.
  > Customer said 'Run the stress test. I want to see CPU temps and brightness after 30 minutes of dual-app load. If it holds up, I'm sold.' Salesperson responded with real-time data: '18 minutes in: CPU holding at 42°C, brightness locked at 100%, zero throttling.'
- 第7轮 [Pain point summary and assumptive close]：Reinforced the value proposition by linking back to the customer's stated pain points, then used an assumptive close to move to action.
  > Salesperson said 'So your laptop's fan noise and battery anxiety killed your focus... the MatePad Air breaks that cycle completely. You've seen it handle your workflow without lag or throttling. Let's lock this in: I'll pull up the order form...'

### 失效操作
- 第2轮 [Vague battery claim without evidence]：The response was partially in Chinese (violating language constraint) and lacked concrete evidence, which could have undermined credibility with a technical customer.
  > Salesperson said '10,100mAh battery easily gives you a full day... 10 to 12 hours screen-on time. Plus, 66W SuperCharge refuels to 准确耗电数据我现场拿真机给您实测 in just 30 minutes.' The phrase '准确耗电数据我现场拿真机给您实测' is a mix of Chinese and English, and the salesperson did not actually provide a live measurement.
- 第3轮 [Repeating battery claim without new data]：The customer asked for actual drain rate and thermal behavior; the salesperson only repeated general specs, which could be seen as evasive.
  > Salesperson said 'With both apps live, the MatePad Air stays cool — no thermal throttling or screen dimming even after an hour... The 10,100mAh battery holds steady, giving you 10–12 hours of real coding.' This was a repetition of earlier claims without addressing the customer's specific question about drain rate and thermal throttling.

### 销售经验（可写入培训手册）
1. Always provide live, verifiable evidence when a technical customer asks about performance metrics; avoid vague claims.
2. Use assumptive close after demonstrating value and summarizing pain points; it works well when the customer has shown clear buying signals.
3. Maintain language consistency: do not mix languages in responses, especially when quoting technical data.
4. In in-store scenarios, prioritize hands-on demonstrations and real-time tests over verbal explanations.
5. When a customer asks for specific data (e.g., battery drain rate), provide a direct answer or offer to measure it live rather than repeating general specs.

### 更优话术模板
- **How's the battery holding up with both apps running?**
  > Great question. Let me show you the battery monitor right now. We've been running both apps for [X] minutes, and the battery level is at [Y]%. Based on this rate, you can expect [Z] hours of continuous use. Want me to keep it running while we talk?
- **Any thermal throttling or screen dimming after an hour?**
  > Let's test that right now. I'll start a 30-minute timer with both apps open, and we can monitor the CPU temperature and brightness together. If you see any drop, we'll address it. Deal?
- **I'm not sure this can replace my laptop.**
  > I understand. Let's do a side-by-side comparison with your laptop. Plug in your laptop, open the same code editor and terminal, and we'll see which one stays cooler and smoother. You can judge for yourself.

- **Live Stress Test Trust Builder**

  - 使用条件：After demonstrating value and receiving positive feedback.
  - 示例：Salesperson: 'So your main issue was [pain point]. We've just shown that [product] solves it. Let's proceed with the order.'
