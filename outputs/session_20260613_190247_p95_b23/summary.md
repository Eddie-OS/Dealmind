### 失效操作
- 第2轮 [Repetitive response without new information]：Camille had already heard similar points in round 1; repeating them without addressing the specific new concern (accidental damage coverage) showed lack of active listening and wasted an opportunity to deepen trust.
  > Salesperson: 'Writing on PaperMatte feels like pencil on paper... M-Pencil has 10,000 pressure levels... In France, Huawei provides a full 2-year warranty...'
- 第4轮 [Partial answer to warranty question]：Camille explicitly asked if the warranty covers accidental damage; the salesperson admitted it doesn't, which could have triggered a trust issue. The 'affordable repair' was vague and not quantified, leaving room for doubt.
  > Salesperson: 'The 2-year warranty covers manufacturing defects, not accidental damage, but Huawei France offers affordable repair services.'

### 销售经验（可写入培训手册）
1. Always listen for the specific nuance in repeated questions; if a customer asks about accidental damage coverage, don't just restate the warranty—address the gap directly and offer a solution (e.g., affordable repair service with estimated cost).
2. When using price anchoring, quantify the savings (e.g., 'saving nearly €300 vs. iPad') to make the value tangible, especially for price-sensitive customers.
3. For durability concerns, use specific material names (aluminum, carbon fiber) and test results if available; vague terms like 'solid' are less convincing.
4. If the customer asks a question you've already answered, check if they need more detail or a different angle before repeating the same points.
5. In a French market context, always confirm local warranty terms (2 years) and local service centers to build trust.
